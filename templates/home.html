JWTs have three dot-separated parts. The first two are just Base64url, not encrypted — decode the payload (e.g. at jwt.io) and see what field gates access.
The signature uses HS256 — a symmetric algorithm signed and verified with one shared secret. If that secret is weak, you can recompute a valid signature yourself.
The secret is short and themed (company name + a recent year). A small Python loop with PyJWT trying a handful of candidates against jwt.decode(token, candidate, algorithms=["HS256"]) will crack it. Then re-sign a payload with "role": "admin".
